Based on ideas from *Jerks at Work* by Tessa West, this guide helps identify difficult workplace behavior and respond with practical tactics.

## Why This Matters

Difficult coworkers can drain execution speed, trust, and morale. The goal is not to "win" against difficult people. The goal is to protect your effectiveness while keeping behavior accountable.

## Common Types

### 1) Kiss-Up / Kick-Down

#### Signals

- Belittles peers in private or in front of decision-makers.
- Performs helpfulness upward while creating friction downward.
- Targets people with less organizational power.

#### Likely Motivation

Status gain through impression management and control.

#### Response

- Collect facts, not interpretations.
- Identify patterns across multiple incidents.
- Escalate using impact language: effect on delivery, trust, and team health.
- Build allies quietly and professionally.

### 2) Credit Stealer

#### Signals

- Repackages team ideas as personal contributions.
- Speaks first in unstructured group forums.
- Minimizes attribution when outcomes are visible.

#### Response

- Document ownership and contribution early.
- Use clear, public attribution in docs and updates.
- Ask direct clarification questions in meetings.
- Create role clarity before projects start.

### 3) Micromanager

#### Signals

- Treats all tasks as urgent.
- Focuses on low-level details without conveying priorities.
- Requires constant status pings but gives little context.

#### Likely Motivation

Control, anxiety, or low trust in delegation.

#### Response

- Use specific examples, not general labels.
- Set recurring sync cadence and expected decision points.
- Ask for outcomes and success criteria, not just tasks.
- Push for prioritization when everything is "critical."

## Practical Rules That Work

- Set boundaries early and consistently.
- Use neutral, evidence-based language.
- Keep records for important interactions.
- Escalate based on impact, not emotion.
- Protect your long-term trajectory and credibility.

Healthy teams are built by people who enforce standards calmly and consistently.
