Notes inspired by Edmond Lau's *The Effective Engineer*.

## What Makes an Engineer Effective?

Effective engineers consistently produce meaningful results. They optimize for leverage, not activity.

## Principles

### 1. Focus on High-Leverage Work

- Leverage = impact produced / time invested.
- Prioritize work with disproportionate outcomes.
- Avoid spending cycles on low-value churn.

### 2. Optimize for Learning

- Learning compounds over time.
- Seek environments with strong peers and hard problems.
- Build a habit of daily skill acquisition.

### 3. Prioritize Ruthlessly

- Re-evaluate priorities frequently.
- Keep one trusted task system.
- Protect deep work blocks and reduce context switching.

### 4. Increase Iteration Speed

- Fast feedback loops accelerate learning and quality.
- Invest in automation, tooling, and test speed.
- Improve local workflows to reduce friction.

### 5. Measure What You Want to Improve

- Use actionable and responsive metrics.
- Track outcomes that reflect real user or business value.
- Instrument systems enough to diagnose regressions quickly.

## Bottom Line

Engineering effectiveness is not about working more hours. It is about directing effort where it compounds most.
