## 1. Make Peace With the Past

Estimate total lifetime earnings and compare them with current net worth. The gap reveals how much life energy has already been spent.

## 2. Calculate Real Hourly Earnings

Go beyond salary:

- Start with after-tax income.
- Subtract all work-related costs.
- Divide by total time consumed by work (including commute and recovery).

This gives a truer hourly value of your time.

## 3. Track Spending Precisely

Record all expenses and categorize them monthly. Convert spending into "hours of life energy" to evaluate tradeoffs more clearly.

## 4. Ask Three Questions Per Category

- Did this spending provide fulfillment proportional to life energy spent?
- Is this aligned with my values and goals?
- How would this change if I did not need to work for money?

## 5. Visualize Income vs. Expenses

Maintain a visible trend over time. Awareness often improves both spending discipline and income decisions.

## 6. Reduce Wasteful Spending

Direct money toward what genuinely improves your life. Cut recurring expenses that deliver little value.

## 7. Increase Earning Capacity

Treat earning power as a strategic lever. Upskill, negotiate, and expand optionality.

## 8. Track the Crossover Point

Financial independence begins when investment income can cover core living expenses.

## 9. Invest Intentionally

Build a long-term allocation strategy that balances growth, risk, and resilience. Revisit assumptions as market conditions and personal goals evolve.

## Closing Thought

The core idea is simple: treat money as stored life energy. Spend and invest in ways that increase freedom over time.
