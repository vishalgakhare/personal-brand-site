Reference: Tanya Reilly, *The Staff Engineer's Path*.

## Core Pillars of the Staff IC Role

There is extensive guidance for people management, but staff-level IC expectations are often ambiguous. A practical framing is three pillars:

1. **Big-picture thinking**: Understand context and long-term consequences.
2. **Execution**: Deliver through ambiguity, dependencies, and influence.
3. **Leveling up others**: Raise quality through mentoring, standards, and example.

These pillars rest on strong technical depth and experience.

## What Staff Engineers Actually Do

- Translate business needs into durable technical direction.
- Maintain quality bar while programs move quickly.
- Resolve cross-team ambiguity and integration risk.
- Create alignment without formal authority.

Staff roles are contextual. You must define scope explicitly with your manager and peers.

## The Three Maps

1. **Locator map**: Where your team sits in the broader system.
2. **Treasure map**: Why the destination matters and what value it unlocks.
3. **Topographical map**: Real path, constraints, and tradeoffs along the way.

These maps are shaped by organizational culture and decision-making paths.

## Building a Technical Strategy

A strong strategy includes:

- **Diagnosis**: What matters beneath the noise.
- **Guiding policy**: Principles for hard decisions.
- **Coherent actions**: Sequenced technical and organizational steps.

Write, socialize, and revise until the strategy is clear enough that teams can execute independently.

## Working Principles

- Be explicit about priorities and tradeoffs.
- Invest in relationships across teams.
- Develop strong written communication.
- Frame work in terms stakeholders care about.
- Share credit and grow other engineers.

Staff engineering is less about heroic output and more about sustained system-level impact.
